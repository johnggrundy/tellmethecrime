# Tell Me The Crime — canonical episode script standard

**Approved format: Episode 40, “The Mother Who Never Came Home” (Mamta Kafle Bhatt), revised November 20, 2026.**

Use this standard for **all future TMTC scripts**. Earlier compressed, fact-dense outlines and the previous episode 39–41 drafts are not the preferred model. The approved Mamta version has the right balance of narrative, factual specificity, investigation, human psychology, and interactive discussion.

## Primary purpose

TMTC is a conversation between two hosts, **John and Febriana**, in which one **tells** a resolved true-crime story and the other **hears it for the first time**. The listener must be able to react, predict, question evidence, and discuss mechanisms *before* critical revelations. The script should feel like an unfolding story, never a research summary being read aloud.

## Mandatory document layout

- Delivery: **editable DOCX**, US Letter (8.5 x 11 in), **two text columns** across recording pages, compact readable paragraphs, no unwieldy page turning.
- Match the approved Mamta script's visual hierarchy: dark navy title, muted grey recording instructions and host-only notes, dark blue scene headings, **blue bold DISCUSS WITH [LISTENER]** prompts.
- In the approved document: body uses Aptos ~10 pt, title Aptos Display ~18.5 pt, section headings ~11 pt, prompts ~10 pt bold; recording margins about 0.67 in, with a ~0.24 in column gap.
- Roughly **four recording pages plus a separate sources/research page** when the material supports it. Let meaningful case detail determine length; do not cut facts just to satisfy a word quota.
- Recording pages need a concise title, episode number, **who tells whom**, recording guidance, opening hook, numbered scene headings, narration, and discussion prompts. Mark any evidence warnings as **HOST ONLY** (never spoken). Keep sources on a separate last page.
- Source/research page may be single-column, with direct source URLs and notes for checking disputed claims.

## Structure: follow the approved Mamta episode

1. **Opening hook:** approximately 15–20 seconds in ordinary episodes, but prioritize John's **exact approved hook** when given. Create curiosity without disclosing the decisive action, killer, forensic result, or final confession. Do not rewrite an approved hook.
2. **Opening scene:** start with a real moment, person, place, and immediate question. Let the listener feel the mystery before introducing a timeline.
3. **People and stakes:** establish what the people wanted, their relationships and their routines; include details that make later decisions understandable.
4. **Early warning signs:** narrate relationships, conflict, psychology-relevant behaviour and explanations in chronological order; preserve competing accounts and attribution.
5. **Evidence accumulates:** build concrete details one at a time, not a list of charges, dates or clues. Allow the listener to consider alternate explanations before revealing new evidence.
6. **Turning point and revelation:** place the important disclosure after its setup; avoid telegraphing a verdict or confession.
7. **Outcome and interpretation:** give the verified legal result and remaining unknowns. Discuss evidentiary limits, uncertainty and psychological implications.

**Do not omit motive evidence for brevity.** Research specifically why people acted as alleged: relationship history, jealousy, threats, financial stakes, custody, deception, court testimony, prior conduct, relevant messages, searches, and the prosecution's actual motive theory. Present in context, with who said it and when. Distinguish documented facts, testimony, prosecutor's inference, defence claims, and unresolved possibilities.

## Discussion and psychology

- Aim for about **5–8 substantial DISCUSS WITH [LISTENER]** moments, not decorative [PAUSE] markers. Place them **before** the next major discovery so the listener can genuinely hypothesize.
- Ask specific, open questions about **motive, control, coercion, deception, decision making, circumstantial evidence, investigative interpretation, credibility, and closure**, where supported by that case.
- Do not reveal the answer inside the question. Do not script the listener's response. Let both hosts disagree and reason about evidence.
- Psychology is a conversation, **not diagnosis**. Distinguish a possible mechanism from conclusions justified by the evidence; avoid pop-psychology labels and unsupported psychiatric explanations.
- Name the actual listener: **DISCUSS WITH FEB** when **John tells Feb**, or **DISCUSS WITH JOHN** when **Feb tells John**. NEVER confuse storyteller and listener.

## Evidence discipline

- Ground consequential dates, ages, message wording, quotes, forensic details, legal outcomes, timelines, and relationship allegations in high-quality primary sources or well-identified reporting.
- Explicitly distinguish what investigators found from what suspects said happened. Host-only notes can clarify ambiguity without spoiling the on-air narrative.
- Include exact corroborated specifics that matter (e.g., threats and their surrounding conversation, claimed romantic contacts and what does/does not establish an affair).
- Don't fabricate a definitive motive; explain the **prosecution theory** and competing interpretations.
- Show verdict date and sentencing status **as of the last fact check**. Never claim an unconfirmed future result.
- Keep a **selected-source appendix** with direct URLs and a brief note of what each source substantiates.

## Website and Buzzsprout

- Standard title format: **Episode N: Intriguing Title - The [Case] Case** when case suffix is actually used by the release; use the **exact released title** rather than inventing a suffix.
- Buzzsprout description and website teaser are **spoiler-light** and accurately identify who tells whom.
- **Never invent the Buzzsprout episode ID, URL, release date, or published status.** Check Buzzsprout before updating the live site's latest-episode player and episode cards.
- Keep the homepage latest episode, episodes archive, dedicated episode page, and every older page's latest-episode strip consistent.
- Host John has final authority over exact hook language. Respect approved lines verbatim; a script correction should not silently rewrite the release description.

## Quality gate before delivery

Read the entire script aloud mentally. Confirm: engaging scene transitions; psychologically meaningful discussion; nothing important about motive omitted; the listener can be surprised; sources are adequate; narrator/listener labels are correct; hook has no spoilers; two-column DOCX renders cleanly on every page. **No draft is final until that visual and factual check is done.**
